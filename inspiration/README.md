# Inspiration

This directory collects source material that Group Explorer may learn from later. For now the purpose is simply to keep useful explanations visible and close to the project; nothing here has yet been incorporated into Group Explorer's mathematical or interaction design.

Every work remains the work of its credited author or authors. The copies here are intended to be unmodified source copies, with the original source recorded. Inclusion does not transfer copyright, imply endorsement, or assert a broader license than the author or publisher actually grants.

### T. J. Sullivan — *The First Isomorphism Theorem*

- File: `sullivan-first-isomorphism-theorem.pdf`
- Author: T. J. Sullivan
- Source: https://www.tjsullivan.org.uk/pdf/morphthm2014.pdf
- This is the cows-and-pens explanation: cows are grouped into pens according to the market to which they are sent, before the same idea is transferred to the isomorphism theorem.

## Spectral sequences

### Ravi Vakil — *Spectral Sequences: Friend or Foe?*

- File: `vakil-spectral-sequences-friend-or-foe.pdf`
- Author: Ravi Vakil
- Date on the notes: March 12, 2008.
- Source: https://math.stanford.edu/~vakil/0708-216/216ss.pdf
- This is the note containing Vakil's joke about spectral sequences being scary like spectres, immediately followed by the admission that he had just made the interpretation up.

### Allen Hatcher — *Spectral Sequences*

- File: `hatcher-spectral-sequences.pdf`
- Author: Allen Hatcher
- Source: https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATch5.pdf
- Context: preliminary/incomplete extra Chapter 5 for *Algebraic Topology*.
- Hatcher's site states that paper or electronic copies for noncommercial use may be made freely without explicit permission from the author; all other rights are reserved.
- Thanks to Allen Hatcher for making the book and additional chapter freely available from his Cornell site.

## Rights and provenance

- Keep vendored PDFs unmodified. If we later annotate, excerpt, redraw, or adapt something, put that derivative work in a different file and credit the exact source beside it.
- Record the canonical or author-hosted source even when a local copy is present.
- Attribution is not a substitute for a license. No broader permission is asserted merely because a PDF was publicly downloadable.
- MIT OpenCourseWare material above is attributed under MIT's stated CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 terms unless the individual resource states otherwise.
- Hatcher's stated permission for noncommercial paper/electronic copying is recorded above.
- For the remaining author-hosted works, this index records provenance but does not claim permissions beyond those granted by the relevant author or publisher. If an author or publisher prefers linking over mirroring, remove the local copy and retain the source citation and thanks.

The point of this directory is deliberately generous provenance: when an idea eventually reaches Group Explorer, we should be able to trace not only the theorem but the particular explanation, picture, analogy, lecture, mentor, editor, host, or course that helped make it understandable.
